## <ins>The Project: DriverPass, what was it? Who was the Client? What type of system did they need designed? </ins>
To summarize, DriverPass was our main project where we started from square one with our client interview and built out the system requirements as well as our UML diagrams and modeling to convey to the clients, Liam and Ian, how our system would meet their needs. DriverPass was our clients vision and desired system to capture a void in the market in which their primary customer base would be students who are getting ready for their driving test and need to practice, both on the road and in a classroom environment. The ideal system was an online platform that allowed users to sign up for training and road tests as well as manage their scheduled trainings and receive updates from the DMV to ensure their information was accurate and up to date, ideally this system always online as it's cloud hosted and once the system is complete it would help them capture a large market gap.

## <ins>What I feel I did well: </ins>
I think that thanks to the lessons and content throughout this course I was able to successfully identify the system requirements and needs from the clients and our interactions, helping tie together their vision and needs with a functional design of a system by iterating on the system framework and architecture as well as identifying areas that the client identified as a necessity or requirement that would need additional analysis or conversations, for instance the client's desire to have the ability to at any point pull logs and diagnostic information, this would require an additional layer of data flow to ensure that this information could be available at any point to the client. I feel that my created diagrams and analysis really homed in on the needs of the client and are easily understood at a high level to help facilitate further discussion on the system design.

## <ins>What I would Improve / Revise: </ins>
If I had to choose one part of the project to revise I think I'd try and improve some of the UML diagrams, I could definitely have added some depth and improved the readability, some of my diagrams feel a bit cluttered and although the details are there and the information is included; it's not super beneficial if it's presented in a way that isn't easily understood at first glance, the diagrams shouldn't require a lot of time and effort to understand and I can see how some of the diagrams I made are not quite where I'd like them to be.

## <ins>Interpreting the User's needs and implementing them into the design: </ins>
The way that I interpreted the user's needs from the interview transcript and implemented them into the system was that I looked for key aspects or ideas of the design that would need to be it's own function, process or entity; I then created diagrams and use cases / flows to help depict these needs for implementation, things like account creation or modifying schedules initially seem like standard tasks but in the realm of system design these could require entirely new processes and flows of data through functions or aspects of the system that otherwise weren't a consideration or not needed. Finding what the use cases are for the users is the best way to interpret their needs, this helps find out exactly what's needed of the system based on what the average use is going to use it for and can help ensure their needs get implemented completely.

## <ins>My Software design Approach:</ins>
My approach for designing software often involves sketches and writing out in plain text what the code needs to accomplish, I'll often start with a flowchart or sorts and draw out the logic or layout and combine them into a more complete sketch or diagram before coding anything, I then take this idea sketch and write out placeholder code to get the skeleton down before I go deeper into the functions or classes and get into the minutia of each decision point or the logical flow. I think that some of the techniques and strategies that I've learned throughout the duration of this course that I'm going to use moving forward in the future for designing and analyzing a system or platform will be the iterative approach, I found that using smaller and more repetitive iterations helped ensure that the good logic sticks and the areas that may pose an issue often stand out after the first or second iteration. Using an iterative approach also helps ensure that the design is complete and cohesive, as well as correct, thus helping to guarantee that in the end I'm not left with a garbled mess of a design, but instead with one that makes sense and has a well thought out and tested design.
